The Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Committee raised the base rate by 25 basis points to an annual 3.00% at its August 27 meeting, its second consecutive quarter-point increase after an identical move in July. The decision unwinds the last of the easing delivered in 2024–2025 and puts the policy rate back where it stood in November 2024.

## Fourteen Months on Hold, Then Two Hikes in Two Meetings

The August decision caps a clean round trip in Korean monetary policy. Over the twenty-four months through August 2026, the committee changed rates six times. It cut four times in quick succession — October 2024 (to 3.25%), November 2024 (to 3.00%), February 2025 (to 2.75%) and May 2025 (to 2.50%) — then left the rate untouched for roughly fourteen months. The reversal came in July 2026 with a 25-basis-point increase to 2.75%, followed by the August move to 3.00%.

That sequencing matters more than the headline level. A central bank that hikes at back-to-back meetings, rather than pausing between moves, is signaling urgency that a single adjustment would not. The cumulative 50 basis points over two months matches the pace of the fastest stretch of the prior cutting cycle, run in the opposite direction.

## Market Rates Had Already Moved Ahead of the Decision

Money-market pricing on the decision date shows how much of the tightening had been absorbed in advance. The overnight call rate stood at 3.01%, essentially at the new policy level. The 91-day CD rate was at 3.12%, and the three-year Korean Treasury bond yielded 3.75% — a 75-basis-point premium over the new base rate, suggesting bond investors were positioned for policy to stay at or above current levels over the medium term rather than reverse quickly.

## What It Means for Household Borrowers

The most direct channel to households runs through mortgage pricing. The average rate on newly extended home-purchase loans was an annual 4.48% as of July 2026 — a figure that predates the August hike and already reflected the July increase only partially, given how new-lending averages lag policy moves. With the base rate now 25 basis points higher, new borrowers can expect that average to drift upward in the months ahead, widening the gap between what households paid during the 2.50% trough and what current buyers face.

## The Body Behind the Decision

The Monetary Policy Committee that made the call is a seven-member body chaired by the Bank of Korea governor, with the senior deputy governor serving ex officio. The remaining five members are appointed by the president on the recommendation of five institutions: the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the Bank of Korea governor, the Financial Services Commission, the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry, and the Korea Federation of Banks. Members serve four-year terms, and decisions require at least five members present with a majority of those attending in favor.

The committee has functioned since the Bank of Korea's founding in 1950, and a 2018 amendment to the Bank of Korea Act staggered the terms of two recommended seats — trimming their first post-amendment terms to three years — to prevent too many members rotating out at once. That structural continuity is worth keeping in mind when reading the current tightening phase: the same institution that judged 2.50% appropriate through mid-2026 has now concluded, twice in two months, that conditions warrant a higher rate.

## The Level to Watch From Here

At 3.00%, policy sits exactly where it did before the February 2025 cut, and 50 basis points below the 3.25% level of October 2024. Whether the committee treats the current rate as a stopping point or a waypoint will show up first in the same market prices that anticipated this move — the call rate, CD pricing, and the three-year bond's premium over the policy rate.
