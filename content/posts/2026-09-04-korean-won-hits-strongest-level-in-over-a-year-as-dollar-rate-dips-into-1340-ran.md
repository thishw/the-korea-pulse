The Korean won pushed to its strongest intraday level against the US dollar in more than a year, with the won-dollar exchange rate dipping into the 1,340-won range during the session. The move takes the currency below the 1,350 line for the first time in an extended stretch, a threshold it had failed to breach throughout the won's long run of weakness.

## Reading the 1,340 Handle

The level was recorded during intraday trading rather than at the close, and a single session's dip below a round number does not by itself establish a trend. Even so, a return to the 1,340 range after more than a year on the weaker side of 1,350 narrows the distance to where the currency traded before its prolonged slide, and shifts the reference points dealers and corporate treasurers work with day to day.

## Where a Firmer Won Bites First

A stronger won feeds through the economy along familiar channels. Dollar-invoiced energy and raw-material imports become cheaper in won terms, which tends to ease import-price pressure and, with a lag, consumer inflation. For exporters, the arithmetic runs the other way: dollar revenue converts into fewer won, trimming reported earnings even before any competitiveness effects show up in order books. Foreign holders of Korean stocks and bonds, meanwhile, pick up a currency gain on top of local returns, a combination that can reinforce inflows when the won is appreciating.

## The Open Question

What pushed the rate lower in this particular session — and whether the move extends beyond it — will hinge on the usual mix of dollar direction, trade flows and interest-rate expectations at home and abroad. The more durable test is the close: sustained trade below 1,350, rather than an intraday touch, is what would mark a genuine regime change for the currency.
