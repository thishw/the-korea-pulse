South Korea will add another round of Chuseok cost-of-living support this week, according to Lee Hyung-il, the nominee for deputy prime minister and minister of finance and economy. Speaking on September 7, Lee — who currently serves as the ministry's first vice minister — said the follow-up package would include discount programs for agricultural, livestock and fisheries products, building on the holiday stabilization measures the ministry has already announced.

## Topping Up the Holiday Package

The Ministry of Finance and Economy has already published its Chuseok livelihood stabilization plan, and Lee's remarks confirm the government does not consider that file closed. The additional measures he described center on the most visible pressure point of any Korean holiday season: the cost of table staples. Discounts on farm, livestock and seafood products are the government's standard instrument for blunting holiday food bills, and front-loading a second announcement before the holiday itself suggests the administration wants the relief felt at the checkout counter rather than in retrospective statistics.

## Small Merchants Move First

Business lobbies are not waiting for Lee's confirmation to stake their claims. The Korea Federation of Micro Enterprise met the nominee on September 7 and pressed for an easing of the tax burden on small business owners, among other requests. The timing is deliberate: a minister-designate weighing his first policy commitments is at his most receptive, and small merchants — squeezed between holiday demand and thin margins — want their concerns written into his agenda before it hardens.

## A Nominee Already Running the Desk

Lee occupies an unusual position. As sitting first vice minister, he is helping execute the current relief program; as ministerial nominee, he is previewing decisions he would own after confirmation. His September 7 comments on the timing of new measures read less like a candidate's aspirations than an incumbent's schedule.

The stakes of the handover extend well past the holiday. The ministry is in the middle of compiling the 2027 budget, so whoever is confirmed inherits both the near-term relief file and the government's full spending blueprint. The post Lee has been tapped for — currently held by Koo Yun-cheol — carries deputy-prime-minister rank under the Government Organisation Act, a legacy of the portfolio's weight in Korean economic policymaking since the old Ministry of Finance and the Economic Planning Board were merged in 1994. If confirmed, Lee's first test will be whether the discount programs he announced this week register with the households and merchants they are aimed at.
