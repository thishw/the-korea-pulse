South Korea's most successful retail traders spent the afternoon of September 7 buying the country's two memory-chip giants: Samsung Electronics topped the net-buy list among clients in the top 1% by investment returns at Mirae Asset Securities, with SK Hynix close behind. The striking part is the whiplash — just three days earlier, on September 4, the same cohort had made SK Hynix its single most-sold stock even as it kept accumulating Samsung Electronics. With both chipmakers' shares rising on the 7th, the group chose to chase the move rather than fade it, and funded the shift in part by selling Hyundai Motor and Naver.

## A Three-Session Round Trip on Hynix

The September 4 session showed a clear preference within the memory trade. The top-returning accounts bought Samsung Electronics alongside biotech names Alteogen and PharmaResearch, while unloading SK Hynix, Samsung C&T and SK Square — three names tied to the SK and Samsung group ownership structures — and taking profits on a robotics stock that had jumped roughly 20%. By September 7 that split had collapsed: SK Hynix was back among the heaviest buys as its shares climbed.

A round trip this fast reads less like a change of view on the company and more like tactical positioning around price. SK Hynix sits with Samsung Electronics and Micron as one of the three dominant memory-chip makers, and it posted revenue of roughly 97.15 trillion won in 2025, so the stock functions as the market's purest large-cap expression of the memory cycle. Traders who sold into strength on the 4th appear to have concluded, once the rally extended, that stepping aside was the bigger risk.

## Samsung Electronics as the Constant

Across both sessions the one unbroken thread was Samsung Electronics, which led the cohort's net buying on the 4th and again on the 7th. Holding the top buy slot on a down-leg day for Hynix and on an up-leg day alike suggests these accounts treat Samsung as the core memory position and Hynix as the swing trade around it. Samsung offers the broader business mix — the company was the world's largest IT firm and chipmaker by revenue as far back as 2017 — which may explain why the group's conviction there never wavered even as it churned the rest of the chip complex.

## What Got Sold to Pay for It

The funding side of the trade is as telling as the buying side. On September 7 the cohort's selling concentrated in Hyundai Motor and Naver — a carmaker that produced about 4.15 million vehicles in 2024, down from the prior year, and the dominant domestic web platform founded in 1999. Neither is a distressed name; both are simply outside the semiconductor story that is currently absorbing capital. Combined with the September 4 disposals of Samsung C&T and SK Square, the pattern is a rotation out of autos, platforms and holding-company exposure and into the memory duo, executed in stages.

## Why This Cohort Gets Watched

The data come from Mirae Asset Securities, which ranks as South Korea's largest brokerage by market capitalization — scale it built in part through the 2.39 trillion won acquisition of a 43% stake in Daewoo Securities in January 2016, with the merged firm taking its current name in 2021. Its trading flows from top-percentile accounts have become a shorthand gauge of where the country's most skilled individual investors think the market is headed. Their message this week: the memory rally is the trade, Samsung Electronics is the anchor, and even a stock they sold on Thursday is worth buying back by Sunday if the tape demands it.
