Starting September 9, Korean workers can buy individual investor Korean Treasury Bonds directly inside their retirement pension accounts for the first time, as the government launches a retirement-pension version of its retail bond program and three major distributors open sales the same day. Shinhan Bank, Korea Investment & Securities, and NH Investment & Securities are all accepting orders through defined-contribution (DC) and individual retirement pension (IRP) accounts, extending a savings product that was previously confined to ordinary brokerage channels into the tax-advantaged core of Korea's private pension system.

## Three Distributors, One Launch Date

Shinhan Bank begins selling the 10-year and 20-year individual investor treasury bonds through DC and IRP accounts on September 9. Korea Investment & Securities is running its subscription window from September 9 through September 15, also via IRP and DC accounts. NH Investment & Securities opens its first subscription for the retirement-pension version of the product on the same date. The Ministry of Economy and Finance formally announced the launch of the retirement-pension individual investor treasury bond, making the simultaneous rollout a coordinated policy event rather than a competitive product release by any single firm.

## Why the Pension Wrapper Changes the Product

Individual investor treasury bonds were designed as a buy-and-hold instrument for ordinary savers, and a retirement account is arguably their natural home. DC and IRP accounts are long-horizon vehicles by construction: the money inside them is largely locked up until retirement, which aligns with the 10-year and 20-year maturities now on offer at Shinhan Bank. Placing a government-guaranteed, fixed-income instrument inside these accounts gives pension savers a principal-safe alternative to the deposit products and funds that dominate Korean retirement portfolios — a longstanding concern for policymakers, given how heavily Korean pension assets sit in low-yielding cash-like instruments.

For the government, the channel expansion serves a second purpose: it broadens the retail investor base for Korean government debt at the long end of the curve, cultivating a class of domestic holders whose horizon matches the bonds' maturity.

## What the Subscription Window Will Reveal

The first real data point arrives quickly. Korea Investment & Securities' September 9–15 subscription period will show whether pension savers actually reallocate toward long-dated government bonds when given the chance, or whether inertia keeps retirement money parked where it is. Uptake across the three distributors — a major commercial bank and two large brokerages — will also indicate which channel Korean retirement savers prefer for bond purchases. If demand proves solid, the launch positions individual investor treasury bonds as a standing fixture of the DC and IRP menu rather than a niche product, and would give the finance ministry a template for routing future retail debt issuance through the pension system.
